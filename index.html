Yuming Jin

I am a postdoctoral fellow at the Earth Research Institute, University of California, Santa Barbara. I use atmospheric measurements of oxygen and carbon dioxide — from aircraft campaigns, surface stations, and global transport models — to work out where carbon is going as the climate changes.

Roughly half of the carbon dioxide we emit is taken up by the ocean and the land biosphere, but the split between those two sinks, and how each responds to a warming climate, remains poorly constrained. Atmospheric O2 offers a way in: because photosynthesis and ocean solubility imprint distinct O2:CO2 signatures on the air, precise measurements of both gases can separate the ocean and land contributions. My work develops that constraint — building the observational products, the transport diagnostics, and the coordinate frameworks needed to compare sparse atmospheric data against models on equal terms.

Before UCSB I was an ASP Postdoctoral Fellow at the National Center for Atmospheric Research, working with the Earth Observing Laboratory and the Climate and Global Dynamics division. I completed my Ph.D. in Earth Sciences at the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, UC San Diego, advised by Ralph F. Keeling, where my dissertation was awarded the UCSD Chancellor’s Dissertation Medal.
